Chapter 4

↑ Problem

19. Write $z=a+bi$, so $\overline{z}=a-bi$.

(a) $|\overline{z}|=\sqrt{a^2+(-b)^2}=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}=|z|$.

(b) $z\overline{z}=(a+bi)(a-bi)=a^2-(bi)^2=a^2+b^2=|z|^2$.

(c) By (b), $z\overline{z}=|z|^2$. For $z\neq 0$, $|z|^2\neq 0$, so $z\cdot(\overline{z}/|z|^2)=1$; hence $z^{-1}=\overline{z}/|z|^2$. ∎

↑ Problem

43. Let $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}=\cos(2\pi/n)+i\sin(2\pi/n)$. Then $\zeta^n=1$ and $\zeta^k\neq 1$ for $0<k<n$, so $\zeta$ has order $n$, and the powers $\zeta^0,\zeta^1,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}$ are $n$ distinct points, each with modulus 1 (so in $\mathbb{T}$). A complex number $w$ satisfies $w^n=1$ iff $|w|^n=1$ and $n\arg w\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. iff $w=\zeta^k$ for some $k$; so the set of $n$th roots of unity is exactly $\langle\zeta\rangle=\{\zeta^0,\ldots,\zeta^{n-1}\}$, a cyclic subgroup of $\mathbb{T}$ of order $n$. ∎

↑ Problem

45. Suppose $|z|\neq 1$. For every $k\geq 1$, $|z^k|=|z|^k$. If $|z|>1$ then $|z|^k>1$, and if $|z|<1$ then $|z|^k<1$; either way $|z^k|\neq 1=|1|$, so $z^k\neq 1$. Thus no positive power of $z$ equals $1$, and the order of $z$ is infinite. ∎

↑ Problem

14. Take the pentagon with vertices $1,2,3,4,5$. Rotation $r=$ (1 2 3 4 5); reflection $s=$ (2 5)(3 4) (fixes vertex 1). The 10 elements $r^k$ and $r^k s$ ($k=0,\ldots,4$), in cycle notation: $1=e$; $r=$(1 2 3 4 5); $r^2=$(1 3 5 2 4); $r^3=$(1 4 2 5 3); $r^4=$(1 5 4 3 2); $s=$(2 5)(3 4); $rs=$(1 2)(3 5); $r^2s=$(1 3)(4 5); $r^3s=$(1 4)(2 3); $r^4s=$(1 5)(2 4). Every element is one of $1,r,r^2,r^3,r^4,s,rs,r^2s,r^3s,r^4s$, i.e. a product of $r$ and $s$. ∎

↑ Problem

23. Let $\sigma=(a_0\,a_1\,\cdots\,a_{2k})$ have odd length $m=2k+1$, so $\sigma(a_i)=a_{i+1}$ (indices mod $m$). Then $\sigma^2(a_i)=a_{i+2}$. Since $\gcd(2,m)=1$, the map $i\mapsto i+2\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)$ is a single $m$-cycle, so $\sigma^2$ is one $m$-cycle. (E.g. $\sigma=$(1 2 3 4 5) gives $\sigma^2=$(1 3 5 2 4).) ∎

↑ Problem

25. Every $\alpha\in A_n$ is a product of an even number of transpositions; pair them up. Each pair of transpositions is a product of 3-cycles: if they share a point, $(a\,b)(a\,c)=(a\,c\,b)$; if disjoint, $(a\,b)(c\,d)=(a\,c\,b)(a\,c\,d)$. Multiplying these together writes $\alpha$ as a product of 3-cycles (with $n\geq 3$ there are enough symbols). ∎

↑ Problem

29. Write $D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle$. A rotation $r^k$ is central iff $s r^k=r^k s$, i.e. $r^{-k}=r^k$, i.e. $n\mid 2k$. A reflection $r^k s$ commuting with $r$ forces $r^2=1$, impossible for $n\geq 3$. So: $Z(D_4)=\{1,\,r^2\}$ (order 2, since $4\mid 2k$ at $k=0,2$); $Z(D_5)=\{1\}$ (trivial, $5$ odd); and in general $Z(D_n)=\{1\}$ if $n$ is odd, $\{1,\,r^{n/2}\}$ if $n$ is even. ∎

↑ Problem

34. If $\alpha=\tau_1\tau_2\cdots\tau_m$ is a product of $m$ transpositions, then $\alpha^{-1}=\tau_m\cdots\tau_2\tau_1$ (each $\tau_i^{-1}=\tau_i$), again a product of $m$ transpositions. So $\alpha^{-1}$ has the same parity as $\alpha$: even $\Rightarrow$ even, and yes, odd $\Rightarrow$ odd (equivalently $\mathrm{sgn}(\alpha^{-1})=\mathrm{sgn}(\alpha)$). ∎

Required additional problem

↑ Problem

The order of a permutation is the $\mathrm{lcm}$ of its disjoint-cycle lengths, so an element of order 6 needs cycle lengths whose lcm is 6 - either a 6-cycle, or disjoint 2- and 3-cycles (needing $2+3=5$ symbols).

$\bullet$ $S_4$: only 4 symbols, so no 6-cycle and no disjoint 2+3-cycle; the possible orders are $1,2,3,4$. So $S_4$ has NO element of order 6.

$\bullet$ $S_5$: YES. For example (1 2)(3 4 5) has order $\mathrm{lcm}(2,3)=6$. ∎

Optional challenge

↑ Problem

4.46. Let $z=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\in\mathbb{T}$ with $\theta\in\mathbb{Q}$ (radians), say $\theta=p/q$ in lowest terms, $q>0$. Then $z^k=\cos(k\theta)+i\sin(k\theta)$, and $z^k=1$ iff $k\theta\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, i.e. $kp/q=2\pi m$ for some integer $m$. Since $\pi$ is irrational, $kp/q=2\pi m$ with $k,m$ integers forces $k=m=0$. Hence $z^k\neq 1$ for all $k\geq 1$: the order is infinite. (If instead $\theta$ is a rational number of DEGREES, $z$ has finite order; the point is that $\theta/\pi$ irrational $\Rightarrow$ infinite.) ∎

↑ Problem

5.33. Suppose $\alpha\neq\mathrm{id}$, so $\alpha(i)=j$ with $j\neq i$ for some $i$. As $n\geq 3$, pick $k\notin\{i,j\}$ and let $\beta=(j\,k)$. Then (right to left) $\beta\alpha(i)=\beta(j)=k$, while $\alpha\beta(i)=\alpha(i)=j$ (as $\beta$ fixes $i$). Since $k\neq j$, $\alpha\beta\neq\beta\alpha$, contradiction. So $\alpha=\mathrm{id}$, and the center of $S_n$ is trivial. ∎
